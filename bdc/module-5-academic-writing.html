MODULE 5

Academic Writing and Deep Research Assistance

Synthesise research information, develop structured academic arguments and improve the clarity, coherence and organisation of academic writing with AI as a responsible drafting partner.

BEFORE YOU START

Come prepared for Module 5

  • Bring a rough draft, outline or the key findings/visuals from Modules 1–4 that you want to write up.
  • Decide which citation style your target journal or institution requires (e.g., APA or IEEE).
  • Have a free account ready for at least one citation manager (Zotero is free and works well for beginners).

Disclose AI use where required: Many journals and institutions now require you to declare AI assistance in the manuscript. Check your target journal's policy before submission.

Trainer's Tip, Dr. Shahizan

AI can tighten a sentence, but it cannot tell you if your argument holds up. I always read my AI-assisted draft out loud once before submission. If a paragraph does not make sense when spoken, it usually is not ready yet.

During the preparation of this manuscript, the author(s) used [tool name] to improve the clarity and readability of the text, and [tool name] to assist in identifying related literature. RESPONSIBLE USE

Responsible AI writing checklist

  • Verify every factual claim, statistic and citation the AI drafted or paraphrased.
  • Disclose AI use where the target journal or institution requires it.
  • Never let AI invent citations, data, quotations or findings. Check every reference exists and says what it is cited for.
  • Preserve your own argument, voice and critical judgment; treat AI output as a draft, not a final answer.
  • Run an AI-text or plagiarism checker if required by your journal or supervisor before submission.

EXERCISE 5.3

Verify an AI-drafted paragraph ~15 min

Read the AI-drafted paragraph below as if it were handed to you to include in a manuscript. Before you look at the answer, decide what you would need to check.

Artificial intelligence tools have become widely adopted in higher education, with recent studies showing that over 90% of postgraduate students now use generative AI for their coursework (Chen & Patel, 2024). This dramatic increase has been linked to significant improvements in academic performance, with AI-assisted students scoring on average 30% higher on written assignments (Okafor, 2023). As a result, universities worldwide have begun mandating AI literacy training for all incoming students.
  • Does each citation actually exist, and does the source really say what is claimed?
  • Is each statistic traceable to a specific, checkable source, or does it just sound precise?
  • Does any sentence claim causation ("led to", "resulted in") when the evidence only supports correlation?
  • Is there a sweeping generalisation ("universities worldwide", "all students") that is not actually supported?
Reveal what is wrong with this paragraph
  • "Chen & Patel, 2024" and "Okafor, 2023" read like plausible academic citations but are fabricated for this exercise, exactly the kind of citation an AI tool can invent. Always search for a citation before trusting it.
  • "over 90% of postgraduate students" is a suspiciously round, high figure attributed to unnamed "recent studies" with no traceable source, a classic AI overgeneralisation.
  • "scoring on average 30% higher" implies AI use caused better scores, when at most the invented source could show a correlation. Causation needs a controlled study design, not just an association.
  • "universities worldwide have begun mandating" is a sweeping claim with no source at all, the kind of confident-sounding filler AI text adds to make a paragraph feel authoritative.

EXERCISE 5.4

Convert a reference list between citation styles ~10 min

Reformatting a reference list by hand is slow and error-prone. Use AI to do the mechanical conversion, then verify every entry against the quick reference table above.

  • Take 3 to 5 references from your own reference list, in whatever style they are currently in.
  • Use the ready-to-use prompt below to convert them to your target style (e.g., APA to IEEE, or the reverse).
  • Check every converted reference against the quick reference table above: author order, punctuation, italics and the overall structure.
  • Confirm no reference was dropped, merged or had a detail (year, volume, page numbers) changed or invented.

CITATION CONVERSION PROMPT

Ready-to-use prompt

Act as an academic editor helping me convert a reference list.

Source style: [e.g., APA 7th edition]
Target style: [e.g., IEEE]

References to convert:
[PASTE YOUR REFERENCE LIST HERE]

Convert every reference to the target style, preserving all details exactly as given (authors, year, title, journal or venue, volume, issue, pages, DOI). Do not add, remove or guess any detail that is missing. If a reference is missing information the target style requires, list it separately and flag what is missing instead of inventing it.

Traditional vs AI-assisted academic writing

TaskTraditional approachAI-assisted approach
Improving clarity and grammarManual proofreading and peer review onlyGrammarly or Paperpal suggest edits in real time
Managing citationsFormat each reference by handZotero, Mendeley or EndNote auto-format citations and bibliographies
Finding related work while writingPause writing to search separatelyDeep-research tools surface related literature inline, with sources cited

RECAP

Key takeaways and terms

Key Takeaways

  • Use AI to polish clarity and structure, but keep your own argument, voice and judgement in control.
  • Verify every fact, statistic, quotation and citation an AI drafted or paraphrased. Never assume it is correct.
  • Disclose AI use where your journal or institution requires it.
  • A citation manager (Zotero/Mendeley/EndNote) removes formatting errors so you can focus on the argument.
DOI
Digital Object Identifier, a permanent link to a specific published work.
Plagiarism checker
A tool that compares your text against published sources to flag unoriginal content.
Deep research
An AI feature that searches, reads and synthesises multiple sources into one report.
AI disclosure
A statement in a manuscript declaring where and how AI tools were used.
