ORGANISE · SYNTHESISE · CONNECT

BEFORE YOU START

Come prepared for Module 3

  • Bring 3 to 5 sources, notes or papers you want to organise, either from Module 1's search or your own shortlist.
  • Have a free account ready for at least one mind-mapping tool (e.g., Xmind, MindMeister or Markmap).
  • If using Gemini Notebook Studio, upload your verified sources before the session to save time.

Trace every connection to a source: Whatever tool you use, every theme, cluster or relationship in your map should be traceable back to an actual paper or note, and not invented by the AI.

Trainer's Tip, Dr. Shahizan

A mind map is only useful if you can still explain it a week later. Before you move on, talk through your map out loud to a labmate. If you cannot explain a link between two nodes, it probably needs a source or it needs to go.

EXERCISE 3.2

Build a Research Matrix ~20 min

A research matrix is a table that forces structure onto messy source material or study design, one row per source, question or theme, so patterns, gaps and inconsistencies become visible at a glance instead of staying buried across separate papers or notes. Pick the type that matches what you need right now.

Literature Review Matrix

Organises past studies by author, year, method and findings, one row per source, so you can compare them side by side and spot what the literature has not yet answered.

READY-TO-USE PROMPTS

Copy a prompt for your matrix type

Paste your own sources or study details in place of the bracketed instructions, then run the prompt in your AI chat tool of choice.

LITERATURE REVIEW MATRIX

Ready-to-use prompt

Act as a research assistant helping me build a literature review matrix.

Sources to include (author, year, and a short description or abstract of each):
[PASTE YOUR SOURCES HERE]

Organise them into a table with these columns: Author & Year, Purpose/Objective, Method & Sample, Key Findings, Limitations, Gap or Question This Raises.

Base every cell only on the sources I provided. Do not invent findings, methods or authors. If a source does not report a column's information, write "Not reported" instead of guessing.

After the table, list 2 to 3 possible research gaps suggested by patterns across the rows.

RESEARCH DESIGN MATRIX

Ready-to-use prompt

Act as a research supervisor helping me check the alignment of my study.

My research questions: [LIST YOUR RESEARCH QUESTIONS]
My objectives: [LIST YOUR OBJECTIVES]
My proposed method(s): [LIST YOUR METHODS]

Build a table with one row per research question and these columns: Research Question, Objective, Variables or Constructs, Method, Data Source or Instrument.

Check whether each research question logically aligns with its objective and method. Flag any row where the method does not seem capable of answering the stated question, and explain why.

SYNTHESIS MATRIX

Ready-to-use prompt

Act as a research assistant helping me build a thematic synthesis matrix.

Sources (author, year, and a short description or key points of each):
[PASTE YOUR SOURCES HERE]

Identify 3 to 6 recurring themes across these sources. Build a table with themes as rows and sources as columns, marking which sources discuss each theme and a one-line note on their position.

Ground every theme in the sources provided. Do not invent a theme that is not actually discussed. Note any theme where sources disagree or contradict each other.

DURING THIS EXERCISE

Try it with your own material

  • Pick the matrix type above that matches what you need right now.
  • Paste 3 to 5 of your own sources, or your own research questions, objectives and methods, into a ready-to-use prompt or the custom builder.
  • Run the prompt in your AI chat tool of choice, uploading the actual sources where the tool supports it.
  • Read every row against the real source or your own study design. Correct or remove anything the AI got wrong.
  • Scan across the finished rows for gaps, misalignments or repeated themes, that pattern is the actual output of building a matrix.

A matrix is a thinking tool, not a citation: Every row must trace back to a real source or a real part of your study. If the AI cannot find the information, it should say so, not fill the cell with something plausible-sounding.

EXERCISE 3.3

Concept mapping from an abstract ~15 min

Before you let an AI tool draw a mind map for you, try extracting the concepts and relationships yourself from one abstract. Comparing your manual map against the AI's version shows you exactly what a source-grounded tool adds, and what it might miss.

  • Pick one abstract from your shortlist, ideally one with a few distinct concepts and a clear relationship between them.
  • Manually list the key concepts in the abstract, then write how each pair of concepts relates (causes, is a type of, is measured by, contradicts, and so on).
  • Run the ready-to-use prompt below with the same abstract in an AI chat tool.
  • Compare the two: did the AI find a relationship you missed, or add one that is not actually in the text?

CONCEPT EXTRACTION PROMPT

Ready-to-use prompt

Act as a research assistant helping me build a concept map from a single abstract.

Abstract:
[PASTE THE ABSTRACT HERE]

Extract the key concepts in this abstract, then list how each pair of related concepts connects, as a set of "Concept, Relationship, Concept" triples suitable for a mind map. Ground every triple strictly in the wording of the abstract. Do not add outside knowledge, and do not infer a relationship the abstract does not actually state.

WHY USE AI HERE

Traditional vs AI-assisted knowledge organisation

TaskTraditional approachAI-assisted approach
Clustering themesSort sticky notes or index cards by handKO Prompt Builder or Gemini Notebook Studio clusters themes from your sources in seconds
Drawing the mind mapSketch by hand or manually place nodes one at a timeMarkmap or Xmind render a full map instantly from a text outline
Tracing themes to sourcesFlip between your notes and the map to check each linkA source-grounded prompt keeps every theme traceable to a named source
Updating as new papers arriveRedraw or manually re-cluster the whole mapRe-run the same prompt with the new sources added

The trade-off: AI speeds up structuring; you still confirm every theme and connection is grounded in a real source.

RECAP

Key takeaways and terms

Key Takeaways

  • A synthesis matrix, timeline, mind map or framework map each suit a different organisation goal, so choose based on what you need to show.
  • Always ground AI-generated structure in your actual sources; never let it invent themes or connections.
  • Mind-mapping tools structure your thinking; Gemini Notebook Studio generates a map directly from verified sources.
  • A well-organised literature map becomes the backbone of your literature review section later.
Synthesis matrix
A table organising sources by theme, showing which studies support or address each theme.
Literature matrix
A structured table comparing sources across dimensions such as method, sample and findings.
Node
A single concept or entry in a mind map, connected to related ideas by branches.
Knowledge graph
A network of linked notes, sources or concepts showing how they relate.
