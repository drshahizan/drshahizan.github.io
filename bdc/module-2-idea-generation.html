Check every candidate question against FINER

Feasible

Adequate time, funding, expertise and access to data or participants.

Interesting

Engaging to you, your supervisor and the intended research community.

Novel

Confirms, extends or challenges existing findings rather than repeating them.

Ethical

Approvable by an institutional review board or research ethics committee.

Relevant

Matters to scientific knowledge, practice, or policy in your field.

RECOMMENDED TOOLS

Check whether your idea is genuinely novel

Before committing to a research question, use one of these to see what the literature already says — and whether the assumption behind your idea actually holds up.

01
CITATION CONTEXT

Scite

Shows whether citations support, contrast with or merely mention a publication — useful for testing the assumption behind your idea.

02
RESEARCH LANDSCAPE

ResearchRabbit

Maps related papers, authors and citation connections for iterative exploration of your idea's landscape.

03
LITERATURE MAPPING

Litmaps

Creates citation maps and monitors new publications — a fast way to check whether your idea is already being explored.

04
EVIDENCE SEARCH

Consensus

Synthesises findings across papers and links every answer back to its source — a quick reality check for a candidate idea.

IDEA VALIDATION

Pressure-test your idea with Gemini Notebook

Gemini Notebook builds answers only from documents you upload. Upload the handful of papers behind your idea, then ask it to compare them — a fast way to surface contradictions, gaps and unanswered questions before you commit to a research question.

01

Upload

Upload 3–5 key papers on your topic — your own shortlist, or highly-cited papers found with Scopus AI or Publish or Perish in Module 1.

02

Ask

Ask Gemini Notebook to compare the papers and surface consensus, contradictions and unanswered questions.

03

Refine

Use what surfaces to sharpen your candidate research question and confirm it addresses a genuine gap.

GAP-FINDING PROMPT

Compare your uploaded sources

Compare the uploaded sources on [TOPIC]. Identify: (1) areas of consensus, (2) contradictions or unresolved debates, (3) one specific gap that has not yet been addressed. Use only the uploaded sources, and flag anything uncertain or not supported.

DURING THIS SESSION

Try it with your own shortlist

  • Upload 3–5 papers into a new Gemini Notebook — your own shortlist, or results from Module 1's Scopus AI or Publish or Perish exercise.
  • Paste the gap-finding prompt above and review what Gemini Notebook surfaces.
  • Note one contradiction or gap you did not expect, and consider whether it sharpens your candidate research question.

Verify before you rely on it: Gemini Notebook only reasons over what you upload — it cannot tell you what the wider literature says. Cross-check any claimed gap against a proper search (see Module 1) before treating it as genuinely novel. See Gemini Notebook Prompts for more prompt patterns.
