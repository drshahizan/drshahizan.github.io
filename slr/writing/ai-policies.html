AI Policies of Major Publishers

Where and how nine major publishers expect you to disclose AI use, and what it means when AI is used in a systematic review.

Checked on 17 November 2026. Publisher policies change often, and individual journals may add stricter rules. Always confirm the current Guide for Authors of your target journal before submission. Some summaries were checked against secondary summaries where the official page could not be reached; follow the official link for each publisher.

Common ground

  • AI tools cannot be listed as authors with any of these publishers.
  • Authors are fully responsible for all content, including anything produced with AI.
  • Basic grammar and spelling tools are generally exempt from disclosure; generating or substantially changing content is not.
  • Every AI-suggested reference must be verified against the original source.

What this means for a systematic review

  • AI in the methods (search support, screening prioritisation, extraction, appraisal, coding) is part of how the review was done. Report it in the Methods with tool, version, settings and human verification, whatever the writing-disclosure rule is. PRISMA 2020 item 8 asks you to report automation tools used in study selection.
  • AI in writing follows the publisher’s disclosure rule above.
  • Emerald does not permit generative AI to draft any part of a submission, including the literature review: plan your writing accordingly.
  • Wiley explicitly expects disclosure when AI helps gather references or conduct literature searches.
