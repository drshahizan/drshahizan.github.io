SLR WRITING MODULES · W2

What Journals Expect from an SLR

Contribution, reproducibility and reporting standards that editors check first.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe the standards high-impact journals apply to systematic reviews
  • Justify a new review against existing reviews
  • Recognise the common reasons SLRs are desk-rejected

Key points

Five things editors check first

  • Need: does a recent review already answer this question? What does yours add?
  • Reporting: is the review reported according to PRISMA 2020, with a completed checklist?
  • Reproducibility: can someone rerun the search from the reported strings, sources and dates?
  • Rigour: independent screening, quality appraisal and a justified synthesis method.
  • Contribution: does the review synthesise and interpret, or only describe?

Protocol and registration

  • Registering a protocol (PROSPERO for health-related reviews, OSF for others) shows that methods were planned in advance.
  • If the review was not registered, say so and report deviations from your planned methods.

Common reasons for desk rejection

  • A very similar review was published recently, and the manuscript does not explain the difference.
  • Search strategy incomplete or out of date (often more than 12 months old).
  • No quality appraisal, or appraisal reported but not used.
  • Results that summarise papers one by one.
  • Counts that differ between the abstract, text and PRISMA diagram.
  • Topic outside the journal’s scope, or the journal does not accept unsolicited reviews.

Example: Justifying the review

WeakThis comprehensive review is the first to study AI in education.
StrongerThree previous reviews (2021–2024) examined AI tutors in general; none focused on feedback for novice programmers or appraised study quality. This review addresses both gaps with 42 studies published up to June 2026.
