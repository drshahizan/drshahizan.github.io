SLR WRITING MODULES · W5

Language and Style for SLRs

Tense, hedging, numbers and terminology conventions specific to reviews.

Learning outcomes

  • Use tense consistently for methods, studies and evidence
  • Match the strength of claims to the strength of evidence
  • Report counts and percentages with their denominators

Key points

Tense conventions

  • Past tense for what you did (“We searched…”) and what individual studies did (“Smith et al. found…”).
  • Present tense for what the body of evidence shows (“The evidence suggests…”) and for your interpretation.
  • Present perfect for research activity up to now (“Few studies have examined…”).

Hedging by strength of evidence

  • Many high-quality, consistent studies → “shows”, “consistently reported”.
  • Few or mixed studies → “suggests”, “may”, “evidence is limited”.
  • Avoid “proves”, “all studies” and “clearly demonstrates” in a synthesis.

Numbers

  • Give the denominator: “18 of 42 studies (43%)”.
  • Use the same number of included studies everywhere.
  • Spell out numbers at the start of a sentence (“Forty-two studies…”).

Terminology

  • Choose one term per concept (for example “included studies”, not alternately “papers”, “articles” and “primary studies”) and keep it.
  • Define abbreviations at first use in the abstract and again in the main text.
  • Refer to included studies consistently: by citation or by study ID [S01].

Example: Precision and hedging

WeakRecent years have seen a plethora of studies proving that AI feedback improves learning.
StrongerSince 2022, 31 of the 42 included studies (74%) have examined AI feedback; most reported positive perceptions, but evidence of learning gains remains limited.
