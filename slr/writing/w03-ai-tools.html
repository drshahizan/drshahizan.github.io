AI Tools Across the SLR Workflow

Which tools help at each stage, and what each one should and should not do.

Learning outcomes

  • Match AI tools to each stage of an SLR
  • Distinguish tools that find and rank evidence from tools that generate text
  • Decide when a tool’s use becomes part of the reported method

Key points

Tools by stage

  • Scoping and search: citation-network tools (ResearchRabbit, Connected Papers) and AI search assistants help find seed papers and terms; the final search must still run in bibliographic databases.
  • Screening: Rayyan and ASReview manage screening and can rank records by predicted relevance.
  • Reference management: Zotero, EndNote or Mendeley for de-duplication and citation.
  • Reading and extraction: AI notebooks and summarisers can help locate information in a full text; every extracted value must be checked against the source.
  • Synthesis and visualisation: NVivo or ATLAS.ti for coding; VOSviewer or Bibliometrix for mapping.
  • Writing: language tools such as Grammarly or Paperpal for editing your own text.

What tools should not do

  • Decide final inclusion or exclusion on their own.
  • Replace database searching with an AI chatbot’s answer.
  • Produce citations you have not opened and read.
  • Write the synthesis or conclusions for you.

Choosing a tool

  • Can you report its name, version and settings so others can reproduce the step?
  • Does it keep a log of decisions?
  • What happens to the files you upload?
