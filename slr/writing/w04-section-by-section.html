SLR WRITING MODULES · W4

Writing an SLR Section by Section

What each section of an SLR must contain, from title to conclusion.

Learning outcomes

  • Write each section of an SLR to PRISMA 2020 expectations
  • Decide the order in which to draft the sections
  • Avoid the most common section-level weaknesses

Key points

Title and abstract

  • Identify the report as a systematic review and name the population or context and the focus.
  • Use a structured abstract that follows PRISMA 2020 for Abstracts: objective, sources and date, eligibility, appraisal, synthesis, number of studies, main results, limitations, registration.
  • Write the abstract last.

Introduction

  • Context and problem → what existing reviews cover → the gap → aim and research questions.
  • End with an explicit statement of what this review contributes.

Methods

  • Follow the order of PRISMA items 5–15: eligibility, sources, search, selection, data collection, data items, appraisal, synthesis.
  • Use past tense and report what was actually done, including deviations from the protocol.
  • Put full search strings and long tables in supplementary files.

Results

  • Study selection (PRISMA diagram) → characteristics of included studies → quality appraisal → findings for each research question.
  • Organise findings by theme, not by paper.

Discussion and conclusion

  • Principal findings → comparison with previous reviews → implications → limitations of the evidence and of the review → research agenda.
  • The conclusion answers the research questions and adds nothing new.

Recommended drafting order

  • Methods → Results → Discussion → Introduction → Conclusion → Title and abstract.

Example: Methods

WeakSeveral databases were searched and relevant articles were selected.
StrongerScopus, Web of Science and IEEE Xplore were searched on 15 June 2026 (Supplementary Table S2). Two reviewers independently screened 300 records in Rayyan (κ = 0.81).
