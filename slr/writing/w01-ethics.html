Responsible AI in SLRs: Ethics, Risks and Safeguards

Accountability, confidentiality, failure modes and safeguards when AI touches your review.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain why AI tools cannot be authors of an SLR
  • Identify the SLR tasks where AI use must be reported in the Methods
  • Protect the confidentiality and copyright of the full texts you review
  • Recognise the specific failure modes of AI in evidence synthesis
  • Design checks that catch AI errors

Key points

Authors stay accountable

  • AI tools cannot meet authorship criteria because they cannot take responsibility for the review. Every author must be able to defend every inclusion decision, quality rating and conclusion.
  • In an SLR the stakes are higher than in a primary study: one wrongly included or missed study can change a synthesised conclusion.

Where AI can enter an SLR

  • Search: suggesting synonyms or drafting Boolean strings.
  • Screening: prioritising records (active learning) or suggesting decisions.
  • Extraction and appraisal: pulling data or suggesting quality ratings from full texts.
  • Synthesis and writing: suggesting codes or themes, editing language.
  • Use in the first three is a method and must be reported in the Methods so others can reproduce it; use in writing goes in the AI disclosure statement.

Confidentiality and copyright

  • Many full texts are licensed to your library; uploading them to a public AI tool may breach the licence or the publisher’s terms.
  • Never upload unpublished manuscripts, peer-review material or participants’ data.
  • Check the tool’s data policy: does it train on your inputs? Can you switch that off?

Failure modes

  • Fabricated studies and references: chatbots can produce plausible but non-existent citations.
  • Missed records: AI search assistants do not search databases exhaustively and cannot replace a documented search.
  • Screening errors: prioritisation tools can miss relevant studies if screening stops too early.
  • Extraction errors: numbers and outcomes can be misread or invented.
  • Bias: tools may favour well-cited, English-language or recent papers.
  • Irreproducibility: outputs change between model versions and sessions.

Safeguards

  • Run and report searches in bibliographic databases.
  • Use a pre-specified stopping rule for AI-prioritised screening and report it.
  • Verify every AI-extracted value against the full text.
  • Check every reference with the reference checker.
  • Record tool versions, dates and prompts.

When not to use AI

  • Making final inclusion decisions.
  • Assigning quality ratings without human appraisal.
  • Writing conclusions or research gaps you have not derived from the evidence.

Transparency

  • Keep an AI log: tool, version, date, task, prompt and how the output was checked.
  • Disclose methodological use (Methods) and writing use (declaration), following the target journal.
  • When in doubt, disclose.
