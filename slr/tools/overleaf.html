Overleaf

An online LaTeX editor with journal templates, co-authoring and a live PDF preview. Many computing and engineering journals (IEEE, ACM, Elsevier, Springer) provide LaTeX templates that open directly in Overleaf.

Purpose: Online LaTeX EditorStage fit: Stage 3, Stage 4Relevance: Core / useful

How it can support an SLR

Use Overleaf when your target journal provides a LaTeX template, or when co-authors already write in LaTeX. Tables with many rows (study characteristics, quality assessment) and long reference lists are easier to keep consistent in LaTeX than in Word.

Plan limits to know (checked 18 November 2026)

  • The free plan allows one collaborator besides the project owner; paid Student and Standard plans raise this, and only higher tiers or an institutional licence remove the cap.
  • Free compiles time out after 10 seconds (premium: 240 seconds). Long SLRs with many figures can hit this; compress images and compile drafts in parts if needed.
  • Free project history covers only the previous 24 hours, so keep your own backups (Menu → Download → Source).
  • Check whether UTM or your institution provides Overleaf Commons before paying.

Plans change. Confirm current limits on the provider’s plans page.

Step-by-step: start your SLR from a journal template

  1. Open the journal’s guide for authors and download or follow its official LaTeX template link.
  2. In Overleaf, select New Project → Templates and search for the journal or publisher (for example “Elsevier”, “IEEE Access”, “Springer Nature”).
  3. Copy your section headings in PRISMA order: Introduction, Methods (protocol, sources, search, selection, quality assessment, extraction, synthesis), Results, Discussion, Conclusion.
  4. Connect your reference library: export a .bib file from Zotero (see Zotero Better BibTeX) and upload it, or use the Zotero/Mendeley integration on paid plans.
  5. Paste your characteristics table from the Study Characteristics Table Builder (LaTeX export) and upload PRISMA and chart images as PDF or PNG.
  6. Use Review → Track changes / Comments for co-author feedback, and recompile before every share.

Common problems

  • Undefined citation “?”: the key in \cite{} does not match the .bib file; recompile twice after adding references.
  • Timeout on the free plan: comment out large figures while drafting, or use draft mode (\documentclass[draft]{…}).
  • Template conflicts: do not add packages the journal template forbids; read the template’s comments.
  • Word-only journals: some journals accept LaTeX only as PDF plus source files; check before writing.
