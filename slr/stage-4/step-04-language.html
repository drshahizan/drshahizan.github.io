Key guidance

  • Create a short terminology list (preferred terms, abbreviations defined at first use) and apply it throughout.
  • Use past tense for what the included studies did and found; present tense for what the evidence suggests.
  • Calibrate claims to the evidence: “suggests”, “is associated with” and “indicates” instead of “proves”.
  • Accept language-tool suggestions one by one. Reject changes that alter technical meaning or citations.
  • Read the paper aloud or in a different format (printed / PDF) to catch errors you no longer see on screen.
Needs revision

Over-claimed: “AI feedback improves programming performance.”

Stronger

Calibrated: “Two small pilot studies reported higher immediate test scores with AI hints, but no study assessed retention, so effects on learning remain uncertain.”
