Quality assessment: what to plan in your protocol

Quality assessment (QA) is a structured check of how much confidence to place in the methods and findings of studies that meet your eligibility criteria. It usually informs interpretation and synthesis; it is a separate decision from whether a study is relevant enough to include.

Choose an approach that fits your evidence. Match the appraisal checklist or risk-of-bias tool to your study designs and review question. PRISMA helps report a review; it is not itself a quality-assessment checklist. Do not copy another review’s score threshold without explaining why it suits your own review.

How to read this comparison: the examples use different numbers of questions, scales and cutoffs. Their total scores are not directly comparable. The examples show how authors report a decision rule; they do not establish a universal QA threshold.

Write these decisions into Protocol Item 11

Tool and rationale. Name the checklist/framework and explain why it fits your included study designs.
Domains or questions. State what you will assess, such as sampling, measurement, analysis or reporting.
Rating and decision rule. Define each response, any score calculation, threshold and how you chose it.
Reviewers and process. Specify independent assessors, pilot/calibration, and how disagreements will be resolved.
Use of appraisal. Say whether QA affects inclusion, sensitivity analysis, grouping or interpretation; give your rationale.

Practical sequence: pilot the checklist on a few eligible papers, clarify ambiguous criteria, assess the full set consistently, then report item-level results and explain how they shaped the synthesis. Avoid hiding important weaknesses inside a single total score.
