Stage 1 · Build and check an initial article set

Use the articles as seed examples for learning how to inspect scholarly records. They do not replace a reproducible search in the databases selected for your own topic.

Try this

  1. Sort the titles into: explicitly systematic review, review type needs checking, and comparative or analysis study.
  2. Select two seed papers relevant to your own topic and inspect their abstracts, keywords and reference lists.
  3. Verify title, authors, year, source, volume/issue/pages or article number, and DOI against the publisher or index record.

Save in your Stage 1 portfolio

  1. Record why each seed paper is relevant to your question.
  2. Note terms or synonyms that might improve your keyword matrix.
  3. Record the database/source and date when you retrieve a record. Do not report this curated list as search results.

Stage 2 · Calibrate screening and pilot extraction

For a short title-screening calibration, use this illustrative question: “Does the publication title explicitly identify a systematic review method?” This narrow question is only a classroom exercise; it is not an eligibility protocol for your own review.

Screen in stages

  1. Make a provisional title-level decision: Include, Exclude or Uncertain.
  2. Discuss which words in the title support the decision.
  3. Open the abstract and methods before confirming review type or final eligibility. A title alone is not enough for full-text screening.

Pilot the extraction form

  1. Extract citation fields first: title, authors, year, source, volume/issue/pages or article number, and DOI.
  2. Then check the full text for review aim, databases searched, date range, eligibility criteria and synthesis method.
  3. Leave any unreported field blank or mark it “not reported”; do not infer it from the title.
Keep the sets separate. Stage 2's synthetic practice CSV remains the consistent dataset for duplicate removal and PRISMA count exercises. These real publications are a separate calibration and extraction example. Do not add them to synthetic flow counts.
