3. Essential Knowledge — Six Concepts

Read these short explanations before starting. Open each topic to view the practical example.

01 What makes a review systematic?

An SLR uses a planned, transparent and reproducible method for finding, selecting and synthesising evidence. It differs from a narrative overview by documenting decisions and search procedures.

02 Topic, scope and research questions

A topic identifies a broad field. Scope defines the review boundaries; RQs specify the evidence the review needs to answer. Example: from “AI in cybersecurity” to “Explainable AI for intrusion detection in IoT networks.”

03 Keywords, concepts and synonyms

Break an RQ into core concepts and identify synonyms, spelling variations and acronyms. For example: “explainable artificial intelligence”, “explainable AI”, XAI and explainability.

04 Boolean logic and search syntax

Use OR for alternatives within a concept and AND to combine different concepts. Use parentheses to group terms. Phrase searching, truncation and field codes differ by database.

05 Academic databases and coverage

Scopus, Web of Science and IEEE Xplore have different disciplinary coverage and search syntax. Choose sources for your topic and document the reason. Access may depend on institutional subscriptions.

06 Metadata, exports and search records

Article metadata can include title, authors, year, abstract, DOI and source. Keep the exact search string, database, search date, filters and result count. Export records in a supported format, such as RIS, BibTeX or CSV.
