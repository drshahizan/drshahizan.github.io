Key guidance

Gap typeSignal in the evidenceExample statement
Evidence gapContradictory or inconclusive findings“Effects on learning outcomes are inconsistent across 6 studies.”
Knowledge gapA topic or relationship is rarely studied“Only 2 of 42 studies examine long-term retention.”
Methodological gapWeak or homogeneous designs“31 studies were single-site pilots without control groups.”
Population / contextual gapUnder-represented groups, settings or regions“No study was conducted in Southeast Asian institutions.”
Theoretical gapAbsent or untested theoretical frameworks“Few studies ground their design in a learning theory.”
Practical / empirical gapReal-world deployment or evaluation is missing“Tools were evaluated in labs, not in production settings.”
  • A gap is a finding of your review: it must be supported by counts or patterns from the included studies, not by assumption.
  • Prioritise gaps by importance and feasibility. Three to five well-argued gaps are stronger than a long list.
  • Turn each gap into a future research question or study design (who, what, how, measured by what).
