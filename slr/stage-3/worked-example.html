SLR WRITING · STAGE 3

Worked Example: From Topic to Abstract

One synthetic systematic review on AI feedback for novice programmers, followed through all eleven steps, with links to the matching guide on this site.

This is a synthetic review created for training. The studies, counts and results are invented and must not be cited. Use it to see how each stage connects to the next.

  1. Topic and gap

    Site guide: Stage 1 · Step 1–2 →

    Topic: AI-generated feedback for novice programmers.

    Gap: Earlier reviews covered AI tutors in general; none focused on feedback in introductory programming or appraised study quality, and many studies have appeared since generative AI became widely available.

  2. Research questions (PICOC)

    Site guide: Stage 1 →

    PopulationNovice programmers in higher education
    InterventionAI-generated feedback (hints, explanations, error messages)
    ComparisonNo AI feedback, or human feedback (where available)
    OutcomesPerformance, help-seeking, perceptions
    ContextIntroductory programming courses

    RQ1 What types of AI feedback have been studied? RQ2 What effects on performance and help-seeking are reported? RQ3 What are the limitations of the current evidence?

  3. Search strategy

    Site guide: Stage 1 · Search strings →

    Scopus: TITLE-ABS-KEY ( ("artificial intelligence" OR "large language model*" OR LLM OR ChatGPT OR "automated feedback")
  AND (feedback OR hint* OR explanation*)
  AND (novice* OR "introductory programming" OR CS1 OR "first-year" ) )
  AND PUBYEAR > 2015 AND PUBYEAR < 2027 AND ( LIMIT-TO ( LANGUAGE , "English" ) )

    Same three concept groups adapted to Web of Science (TS=). Date last searched: 15 June 2026.

  4. Eligibility criteria

    Site guide: Stage 2 · Protocol →

    IncludeExclude
    Empirical studies with novice programmers in higher educationK–12, professional developers
    AI-generated feedback on code or errorsHuman-only or rule-free plagiarism tools
    Reports a performance, behaviour or perception outcomeOpinion papers, system descriptions without evaluation
    Peer-reviewed, English, 2016–2026Theses, preprints, non-English

  5. Screening and PRISMA

    Site guide: Stage 2 · Screening, PRISMA →

    412 records (Scopus 268, WoS 144) → 112 duplicates removed → 300 screened by two reviewers (κ = 0.81) → 220 excluded → 80 full texts assessed → 38 excluded (wrong population 14, no evaluation 12, no relevant outcome 8, not English 4) → 42 studies included.

    See the PRISMA example in the figure gallery.

  6. Quality appraisal

    Site guide: Stage 2 · Step 6 →

    MMAT, five criteria per design, scored 2 / 1 / 0 (maximum 10). Two reviewers appraised each study. 31 studies scored ≥ 60%; the 11 lower-scoring studies were retained but flagged in the synthesis.

  7. Data extraction

    Site guide: Stage 2 · Step 7 →

    Fields: ID, citation, country, setting, design, sample, AI tool, feedback type, outcome measures, main findings, limitations. Form piloted on 5 studies; a second reviewer verified 20% of entries.

  8. Synthesis

    Site guide: Stage 3 · Step 4 →

    RQThemeStudiesStrength
    RQ1Hints, explanations and error reformulation dominate38—
    RQ2Short-term performance gains are mixed17Limited
    RQ2Students value code-specific explanations22Moderate
    RQ2Help-seeking shifts from tutors to AI, with human confirmation9Emerging
    RQ3Single-site, short studies; no retention measures42—

  9. A results paragraph

    Site guide: Stage 3 · Step 7 →

    Evidence on performance was mixed. Of the 17 studies that measured performance, nine reported higher immediate test or exercise scores with AI feedback [S01, S04, …], whereas eight found no significant difference [S02, S06, …]. Positive results came mostly from short controlled pilots, while larger observational studies tended to report no effect. None of the 17 studies measured retention beyond the end of the course.

  10. Gaps and future research

    Site guide: Stage 3 · Step 5 →

    • Knowledge gap: 0 of 42 studies measured retention → Does AI feedback improve retention eight weeks after a course?
    • Methodological gap: 31 of 42 were single-site pilots → Do effects hold in multi-site randomised trials?
    • Context gap: no study from Southeast Asia → How do Malaysian novice programmers use AI feedback?

  11. Structured abstract

    Site guide: Stage 4 · PRISMA for Abstracts →

    Objective: To synthesise evidence on the effects of AI-generated feedback on novice programmers. Methods: We searched Scopus and Web of Science (2016–June 2026). Two reviewers screened records and appraised quality with the MMAT; findings were synthesised thematically. Results: Forty-two studies were included. Students valued code-specific explanations, but evidence of performance gains was mixed and came mainly from short, single-site studies. No study measured retention. Conclusions: AI feedback is well received, but its effect on learning remains uncertain. Registration: OSF (doi …).
