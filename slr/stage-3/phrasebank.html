SLR WRITING · STAGE 3 & 4

SLR Phrasebank

68 phrases for reporting a systematic literature review, organised by section. Search, copy and adapt them to your own review.

Replace each “…” with your own details. A phrase is only a frame: the evidence, numbers and citations must come from your review.

Justifying the review

  • Despite growing interest in …, the evidence on … remains fragmented across …
  • Existing reviews have focused on … but have not examined …
  • The most recent review on this topic (Author, Year) covered studies up to …; since then, …
  • A systematic synthesis is needed to establish what is known about … and where evidence is lacking.
  • To the best of our knowledge, no systematic review has examined … in the context of …

Stating the aim and research questions

  • This systematic literature review aims to identify, appraise and synthesise studies on …
  • The review addresses the following research questions: RQ1 …; RQ2 …; RQ3 …
  • The research questions were structured using the PICo / PICOC framework (Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome, Context).
  • Specifically, this review seeks to determine (1) …, (2) … and (3) …

Protocol, registration and reporting guideline

  • This review was conducted and reported in accordance with the PRISMA 2020 statement (Page et al., 2021).
  • The review protocol was registered with PROSPERO (CRD…) / OSF (doi …) on … before screening began.
  • The protocol was not registered; deviations from the planned methods are reported in Section ….
  • A completed PRISMA 2020 checklist is provided in Supplementary Table S1.

Describing the search

  • We searched Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection and IEEE Xplore from … to … (date last searched: …).
  • The search string combined three concept groups using Boolean operators: (…) AND (…) AND (…).
  • Search terms were developed from the research questions, seed papers and database thesauri, and piloted to test recall.
  • Searches were limited to peer-reviewed journal articles and conference papers published in English between … and ….
  • Backward and forward citation searching of the included studies identified … additional records.
  • The full search strategy for each database is provided in Supplementary Table S2.

Eligibility and study selection

  • Studies were included if they (1) …, (2) … and (3) …; studies were excluded if they ….
  • After removing … duplicates in Zotero, two reviewers independently screened titles and abstracts against the eligibility criteria.
  • Disagreements were resolved through discussion, and a third reviewer was consulted when consensus could not be reached.
  • Inter-rater agreement at the title and abstract stage was substantial (Cohen’s κ = …).
  • Full texts of … potentially eligible records were assessed, and … were excluded with reasons (Figure 1).
  • Rayyan / ASReview was used to manage screening; all automated suggestions were verified by a human reviewer.

Quality appraisal

  • The methodological quality of the included studies was assessed using … (e.g. MMAT, CASP, JBI checklist).
  • Each study was rated on … criteria, scored as 2 (yes), 1 (partly) or 0 (no), giving a maximum score of ….
  • Two reviewers appraised each study independently; disagreements were resolved by discussion.
  • Studies scoring below … were excluded / retained but their findings were interpreted with caution.
  • Quality assessment results for each study are reported in Table 2.

Data extraction

  • Data were extracted using a standardised form piloted on … studies.
  • The following data were extracted: author, year, country, setting, research design, sample, intervention or technique, outcomes and main findings.
  • One reviewer extracted the data and a second reviewer verified …% of the entries.
  • Where information was not reported, it was recorded as “not reported”; authors were not contacted.

Synthesis method

  • Given the heterogeneity of study designs and outcomes, a meta-analysis was not appropriate; a narrative synthesis was conducted following Popay et al. (2006).
  • Findings were synthesised thematically following the three stages described by Thomas and Harden (2008).
  • Studies were grouped by … and findings were compared within and across groups.
  • Quantitative outcomes were summarised using vote counting based on the direction of effect, following the SWiM guideline (Campbell et al., 2020).

Reporting study selection and characteristics

  • The search identified … records; after removing duplicates, … records were screened and … studies were included (Figure 1).
  • Publications increased steadily after …, with … of the … studies (…%) published since ….
  • Most studies were conducted in … (n = …), followed by … (n = …).
  • The majority of studies used … designs (n = …, …%), while only … used ….
  • The characteristics of the included studies are summarised in Table 1.

Reporting synthesised findings

  • Three themes emerged from the synthesis: …, … and ….
  • Evidence on … was mixed: … studies reported …, whereas … found no significant difference.
  • … was the most frequently reported …, appearing in … of the … studies.
  • Across studies, … was consistently associated with …, although most evidence came from ….
  • Only … studies examined …, all of which were …

Signalling strength of evidence

  • These findings should be interpreted with caution, as most evidence came from small, single-site studies.
  • The evidence suggests that … may …, but this is based on … studies of moderate quality.
  • Higher-quality studies tended to report … effects than lower-quality studies.
  • Because few studies measured …, conclusions about … cannot yet be drawn.

Comparing with previous reviews

  • This finding is consistent with an earlier review by …, which reported ….
  • In contrast to the review by …, which focused on …, this review shows that ….
  • Whereas previous reviews were limited to studies published before …, this review includes …
  • This review extends earlier work by examining … and by appraising the quality of the evidence.

Research gaps and future research

  • None of the included studies examined …, which represents an important knowledge gap.
  • … of the … studies were single-site pilots without a control group, highlighting the need for ….
  • Future research should investigate whether … using longitudinal / multi-site designs.
  • No study was conducted in …; research in these contexts would test whether the findings generalise.

Limitations of the review

  • This review was limited to studies published in English, which may have excluded relevant evidence.
  • Grey literature was not searched, so publication bias cannot be ruled out.
  • Although two reviewers screened all records, the possibility that some relevant studies were missed cannot be excluded.
  • The heterogeneity of outcome measures prevented a quantitative synthesis.
  • The search was completed on …; studies published after this date are not included.

Concluding

  • This review shows that …, but evidence for … remains limited.
  • Taken together, the findings indicate that …
  • For practitioners, the evidence suggests that …; for researchers, priorities include … and ….
