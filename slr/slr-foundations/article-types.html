Compare article types

TypePrimary purposeTypical method
Original researchReport findings from a new studyExperiment, survey, interviews or other primary analysis
Narrative reviewDiscuss a body of literatureFlexible selection and narrative interpretation
Systematic literature reviewAnswer focused questions through systematic evidence synthesisPlanned search, eligibility criteria, appraisal when appropriate and transparent synthesis
Scoping reviewMap the extent and nature of evidenceSystematic mapping of concepts, sources and gaps
Bibliometric analysisStudy publication and citation patternsMetadata and network/statistical analysis
Meta-analysisStatistically pool compatible quantitative study resultsEffect-size synthesis, often within a systematic review

A systematic review does not require a meta-analysis, and a bibliometric study is not automatically an SLR.

Practice: Identify the article type

Example A: Researchers recruit participants and measure their task performance. Original research.

Example B: Authors report explicit databases, search dates, screening criteria and a structured synthesis. Likely systematic review; confirm the full methods.

Example C: Authors map co-citation networks and keyword co-occurrence. Bibliometric analysis.
