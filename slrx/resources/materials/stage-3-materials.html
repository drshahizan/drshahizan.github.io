Evidence Synthesis, Research Gaps and Manuscript Writing

Course materials are hosted directly in this website and organised around the 2026 SLR learning journey.

1. From Extraction to Synthesis

Synthesis is more than summarising papers one by one. Compare studies across a common analytical frame and organise the evidence around the research questions.

Narrative synthesis

Use when studies are heterogeneous. Describe patterns, consistencies, contradictions and contextual differences, supported by evidence tables.

Thematic synthesis

Code recurring concepts, group related codes into descriptive themes and develop higher-level analytical themes that answer the review questions.

Quantitative synthesis

Where study designs and measures permit, numerical findings may be combined using an appropriate statistical approach. Do not combine incomparable outcomes merely to produce a pooled result.

2. Identifying Research Gaps

Look for gaps supported by the evidence: under-studied populations or contexts, inconsistent findings, methodological limitations, missing comparisons, limited longitudinal evidence, weak evaluation practices or unresolved theoretical relationships. Discovery tools may assist exploration, but the gap statement must be justified from the included literature.

3. Crafting the Manuscript

SectionCore content
IntroductionContext, problem, motivation, gap, review aim and research questions.
MethodProtocol, databases, search strings, eligibility criteria, screening, quality assessment, extraction and synthesis.
ResultsPRISMA flow, study characteristics and findings organised around the RQs.
DiscussionInterpretation, comparison, implications, gaps, limitations and future research.
ConclusionConcise answer to the review purpose and principal contribution.

4. Tables and Figures

Use tables for study characteristics, quality results, taxonomies and evidence comparisons. Use figures for the PRISMA flow, conceptual relationships, trends or synthesis frameworks. Every visual must be explained in the text and contribute to an RQ.

Hands-on writing task

  1. Map every RQ to extracted evidence.
  2. Create at least one synthesis table.
  3. Draft one evidence-based results subsection.
  4. Write a defensible research-gap paragraph.
  5. Outline Introduction, Method, Results and Discussion.
✓
Stage 3 output

Synthesis structure, core tables/figures, research-gap narrative and first substantial manuscript draft.
