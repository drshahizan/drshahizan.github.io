Search, Discovery and Visualization

Course materials are hosted directly in this website and organised around the 2026 SLR learning journey.

1. Literature Search

A systematic search should be transparent, reproducible and aligned with the research questions. Record the database, exact search string, search date, filters and number of records retrieved.

Tool / DatabasePurpose
ScopusMultidisciplinary abstract and citation database for structured scholarly searching.
Web of ScienceCitation database useful for indexed literature and citation tracing.
Google ScholarBroad discovery across scholarly sources; useful as a supplementary search source.
Semantic ScholarScholarly discovery with semantic and recommendation features.
PubMedBiomedical and life-sciences literature.
Publish or PerishRetrieves and analyses scholarly citation data from supported sources.
Consensus / Elicit / sciteAI-assisted discovery, evidence exploration and citation-context support. Verify outputs against original sources.

2. Paper Discovery

Discovery tools complement—not replace—the formal database search. Use seed papers to explore citation networks and related literature.

ToolUse in SLR
Connected PapersExplore relationships around a seed paper.
LitmapsBuild and monitor literature maps.
ResearchRabbitDiscover related papers and author networks.
IncitefulExplore citation graphs from seed papers.

3. Literature Visualization

Visualization can help participants understand themes and relationships before synthesis. Appropriate tools include VOSviewer, CiteSpace, Gephi and Bibliometrix. Visual patterns are exploratory evidence and must be interpreted alongside the underlying studies.

Hands-on exercise

  1. Define one focused research topic and research question.
  2. Create a keyword and synonym matrix.
  3. Build a Boolean search string using AND, OR and NOT where appropriate.
  4. Run the search in at least two scholarly databases and record the results.
  5. Select one discovery or visualization tool and document what additional papers or themes it reveals.

Research question, keyword matrix, database-ready search strings and initial article set.
