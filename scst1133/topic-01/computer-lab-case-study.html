Computer Laboratory Case Study

Apply system thinking to a university computer laboratory as a complete socio-technical system.

Case context

A faculty laboratory supports teaching, programming, data analysis and online assessment. It includes desktop computers, shared software, authentication, printers, local networking, Internet access and technical support.

Purpose and usersStudents, lecturers, technicians and administrators
Technical componentsComputers, displays, storage, operating systems, applications and network equipment
ProceduresLogin, booking, software installation, backup, maintenance and incident reporting
ConstraintsBudget, capacity, licensing, security, accessibility, energy and support time

Analysis questions

  1. Where should the system boundary be drawn?
  2. What input, processing, storage, output and feedback occur?
  3. Which component could become the principal bottleneck?
  4. What security, privacy and accessibility risks exist?
  5. Recommend one measurable improvement.
