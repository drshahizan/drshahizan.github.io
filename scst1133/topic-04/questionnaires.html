Questionnaires

Reach many people at once—if the questions are clear, unbiased and well designed.

04Questionnaires
Interactive method

Write, scale, design and administer

Questionnaires gather information about people’s attitudes, beliefs, behaviours and characteristics.

When to use a questionnaire

  • People are widely dispersed across locations
  • Many people are involved with the project
  • Exploratory work is needed before interviews
  • Problems should be surfaced before follow-up interviews

Open or closed questions?

Trade-offOpen-endedClosed
Speed of completionSlowFast
Exploratory natureHighLow
Breadth and depthHighLow
Ease of preparationEasyDifficult
Ease of analysisDifficultEasy

Use closed questions when all options can be listed and are mutually exclusive; use open questions to gather opinions.

Language checklist

Questions should be simple, specific, short, free of bias, not patronising, technically accurate and suited to the reading level of respondents who actually know the answer.

Measurement scales

Nominal

Classify

Used to classify things—for example, faculty or vehicle type. It is the weakest form of measurement.

Interval

Equal intervals

Intervals between points are equal, but there is no absolute zero—like temperature in Celsius. Averages can be calculated.

Problems with scales: rater errors

ErrorWhat happensHow to reduce it
LeniencyEasy raters score everything highlyShift the “average” point off-centre so the scale spreads out
Central tendencyRespondents rate everything as averageAdjust descriptor strength or add more scale points
Halo effectAn impression from one question carries into the nextRate one trait at a time across all items, rather than all traits for one item together

Designing and administering

Design

Easy to complete

  • Ample white space and room for answers
  • Easy to mark answers clearly
  • Consistent style
  • Most important questions first
  • Group similar items; less controversial questions early
Administer

Reach the right people

  • Convene respondents together
  • Administer personally
  • Self-administered
  • By mail
  • Over the web or email—lower cost, responses stored electronically

Validity and reliability

Validity is the degree to which a question measures what the analyst intends to measure. Reliability is consistency: the same questionnaire under the same conditions should produce the same results.

Source: Lecture slides, based on Kendall, K. E. & Kendall, J. E. (2020). Systems Analysis and Design (10th ed.). Pearson.
