Stories and Joint Application Design

Listen to the stories an organisation tells, or bring everyone into one room to design together.

03Stories · JAD
Interactive method

Stories reveal culture; JAD builds consensus

Two further interactive methods complement interviewing: listening to organisational stories and running Joint Application Design sessions.

Organisational stories

Stories originate in the workplace and relay information. A story retold over time takes on a mythic quality. Isolated stories are useful for facts; enduring stories capture many aspects of the organisation and are the ones analysts should look for.

PurposeWhat the story does
ExperientialDescribes what the business or industry is like
ExplanatoryExplains why the organisation acted a certain way
ValidatingConvinces people that a decision was correct
PrescriptiveTells the listener how to act

Joint Application Design (JAD)

JAD can replace a series of interviews. Analysts and users work together in a group to analyse requirements and design the user interface.

Use JAD when users want something new, the culture supports joint problem-solving, more ideas are expected from a group, and people can be away from their jobs for the time required.

RoleResponsibility
Executive sponsorUltimate authority for decisions about the project
Session leaderOrganises, schedules and guides sessions; mediates disputes
UsersThe main focus; represent several levels of the organisation
AnalystsDevelop logical models and specifications; may build a prototype
ScribeEnsures results are documented and delivered
ObserversWatch and listen

Sessions usually involve 8 to 12 participants and are often held offsite for 2 to 4 days in comfortable surroundings with minimal distractions, a clear agenda and an orientation meeting.

Benefits and drawbacks of JAD

Benefits

Faster and more owned

  • Saves time compared with many interviews
  • Speeds up development
  • Improves users’ sense of ownership
  • Encourages creative ideas
Drawbacks

Demanding to organise

  • Needs a large block of time from everyone
  • Fails if preparation or follow-up is incomplete
  • The organisation’s culture may not suit it
