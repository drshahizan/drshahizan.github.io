Interviews

An interview reveals opinions, feelings, goals and concerns that documents never show.

02Interviews
Interactive method

Plan, ask, arrange, close and report

Interviewing is one of the most important ways to discover human and system information requirements, including key human–computer interaction (HCI) concerns.

Five steps of interview preparation

Read background material

Learn about the organisation and its vocabulary before you meet anyone.

Establish objectives

Decide what you need to learn from this interview.

Decide whom to interview

Include people at different levels who are affected by the system.

Prepare the interviewee

Contact them in advance, explain the purpose and agree a time.

Decide question types and structure

Choose open or closed questions and how to arrange them.

Two main types of question

Open-ended

Breadth and depth

Interviewees respond however and at whatever length they wish.

“What is your opinion of the current e-learning system, and how could it be improved?”

  • Puts the interviewee at ease
  • Reveals vocabulary and rich detail
  • Opens new avenues of questioning

But: may produce irrelevant detail, take too long or lose control of the interview.

Closed

Precise and efficient

The number of possible responses is limited.

“How many users are registered in your system?”

  • Saves time and gets to the point
  • Easy to compare across interviews
  • Keeps control of the interview

But: can be boring, misses rich detail and builds less rapport.

Other types of question

Bipolar questions are a special kind of closed question answered with “yes/no” or “agree/disagree”. Use them sparingly.

Probing questions draw out more detail, clarify meaning or expand on a point—for example, “Why?” or “Could you explain that a little more?” They can be open or closed.

StructureOrderBest when
PyramidSpecific closed questions → general open questionsThe interviewee needs warming up or seems reluctant
FunnelGeneral open questions → specific closed questionsThe interviewee feels strongly about the topic; offers an easy, non-threatening start
DiamondClosed → open → closedYou want the strengths of both—though it takes longer

Try it · Open-ended or closed?

Select the type for each question.

How do you currently find a parking space on campus?

How many minutes did you spend looking for parking today?

Do you renew your parking permit online? (Yes/No)

What frustrates you most about the permit process?

Which faculty are you from?

Do you agree that enforcement is consistent?

Closing and reporting

Closing: always ask “Is there anything else you would like to add?”, summarise your impressions, ask whom you should talk to next, arrange any follow-up and thank the interviewee.

Interview report: write it as soon as possible, start with a summary followed by detail, and review it with the interviewee.
