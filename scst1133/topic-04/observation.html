Part A · Step 07 · Unobtrusive

Observation and STROBE

What people say they do and what they actually do are not always the same.

07Observation
Unobtrusive method

See what people actually do

Observation shows what organisational members actually do, reveals relationships between decision makers and others, and gives clues about human–computer interaction concerns.

The analyst’s playscript

The analyst observes a decision maker and records their actions using action verbs such as talking, sampling, corresponding and deciding, alongside the information they use.

STROBE: what to observe

ElementExample question
Office locationWho has the corner office? Are key decision makers spread across floors?
Desk placementDoes it encourage communication or demonstrate power?
Stationary equipmentDoes the decision maker store information personally? Is storage large or small?
PropsIs there evidence of a PC, smartphone or tablet in use?
External information sourcesDoes information come from trade journals or the web?
Lighting and colourIs the lighting set for detailed work or casual conversation? Are the colours warm?
ClothingDo decision makers show authority through dress? Are uniforms required?

Comparing observation with interviews

STROBE checks whether the surroundings confirm or contradict what people said in interviews or questionnaires. Five symbols are used:

SymbolMeaning
✓ Check markThe narrative is confirmed
✗ CrossThe narrative is reversed
◉ EyeA cue to look further
□ SquareObservation modifies the narrative
○ CircleObservation supplements the narrative

Try it · STROBE practice

Observe a real office, lab or counter on campus (with permission). Tick each element once you have recorded an observation.

!

Ethics: Always ask permission before observing people at work, and never photograph individuals without consent.
