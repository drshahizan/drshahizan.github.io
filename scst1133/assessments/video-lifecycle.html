Video: The Data Engineering Lifecycle

Explain the data engineering lifecycle through one case study, in a group video of no more than five minutes, presented in your own voices and uploaded to YouTube.

The task

What to explain

Work in your Assignment 1 group (same members and group code, for example S03-G1). Explain how data moves through the data engineering lifecycle, from where it is created to where it delivers value.

The five lifecycle stages (all required)

1 · Generation2 · Storage3 · Ingestion4 · Transformation5 · Serving

Undercurrents (explain at least two)

SecurityData managementDataOpsData architectureOrchestrationSoftware engineering

One case study (required)

Choose one case study from the approved list below and follow its data through all five stages and the undercurrents you choose. Explain what data is created, where it is stored, how it is ingested, how it is transformed and who uses it.

Rules

Video rules

1

No AI voices

Every voice in the video must be the real voice of a group member. Text-to-speech and AI voice tools are not allowed.

2

Everyone speaks

Every member must present a part of the video. Plan about 60–75 seconds for each person.

3

Show the speaker

The speaker's face must be visible while they talk: full screen or as a picture-in-picture box over slides or visuals.

4

Maximum 5 minutes

The whole video, including the introduction and credits, must be 5:00 or shorter.

5

One case study

Apply the lifecycle to one case study: follow its data from generation to serving.

6

Cite what you use

You may use suitable images, diagrams or clips, but credit every source on screen or in the YouTube description.

7

One language only

Choose English or Bahasa Melayu, and use the same language from start to finish, both spoken and on screen.

Penalties. If the video is longer than 5 minutes, only the first 5:00 is assessed. A member who does not speak, or whose part uses an AI voice, receives 0 for Participation and presence.

Plan your video

Suggested structure

A five-minute video goes quickly. Write a script, time it, and rehearse before you record.

0:00–0:20Title card: video title, group code and members' names
0:20–0:50Introduction: the lifecycle and your case study
0:50–3:40The five stages applied to the case study, shared between members
3:40–4:40Undercurrents in the case study and conclusion
4:40–5:00References and credits

Recording tips

  • Record in landscape (16:9), at least 720p, in a quiet room facing a light source.
  • Use picture-in-picture so the speaker stays on screen while slides or diagrams are shown.
  • Show each speaker's name on screen when they start talking.
  • Use only copyright-free music, for example from the YouTube Audio Library, and keep it quieter than your voice.
  • You may use AI to brainstorm or check your script, but say so in the description. Voices and faces must be your own.

Submission rules

Submission, privacy and backup

  • One submission per group. Any member may submit on the website; the group leader submits the same link on UTM e-Learning.
  • You may update your submission until the deadline. After the deadline, do not change, replace or re-upload the video.
  • Consent: every member must agree to appear in the video. If a member is uncomfortable with a public video, choose Unlisted.
  • Privacy: do not show personal data such as IC numbers, phone numbers, addresses or private chats. Use only publicly available information about your case study.
  • Backup: keep the final MP4 file in your group's Google Drive in case YouTube blocks or removes the video.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

Can we record our parts separately and combine them?

Yes. Each member can record their own part; combine them into one video with a consistent style.

Can we use Google Vids?

Yes, Google Vids is recommended. Sign in with your @graduate.utm.my account and share the project with your group members. Record your own voice and camera in Vids. Its AI voiceover and AI avatar features are not allowed.

Can we use AI-generated images?

Yes, but label them on screen (for example, "Image generated with AI") and declare the tool in the YouTube description.

Can two groups use the same case study?

No. Each case in the approved list can be used by one group only. Register it on the submission page; the first group to register it keeps it.

What if a member does not contribute?

Inform the lecturer before the deadline, with evidence such as your storyboard and chat records.

Can the video be shorter than five minutes?

Yes. Five minutes is the maximum, but the video must still cover all five stages, at least two undercurrents and the case study.
