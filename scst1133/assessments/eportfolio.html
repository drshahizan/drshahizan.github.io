01

Purpose of the assignment

Your e-portfolio should use selected work, evidence and reflection to explain how your understanding develops throughout SCST1133. It should function as both an assessment submission and a professional record of your learning.

FoundationsComputing concepts and digital tools
LandscapeData engineering and the data landscape
EcosystemIndustry roles, components and collaboration
LifecycleData acquisition and engineering workflow
ResponsibilityManagement, governance and ethics
InnovationEmerging areas and design thinking
02

Learning outcomes

Your portfolio should provide clear evidence related to the three course learning outcomes.

CLO1 · EXPLAIN

Explain basic information technology concepts and their role in data-driven systems using systems thinking.

CLO2 · CONSTRUCT

Construct a basic data engineering lifecycle using appropriate technologies, practices and industry standards.

CLO3 · DEVELOP

Develop a prototype that addresses the data engineering ecosystem and lifecycle while demonstrating ethical principles and professional responsibility.

03

Required portfolio sections

Include all six sections. You may organise them as separate pages or as clearly labelled sections within one website.

A

Home and professional profile

Introduce yourself as a computing student interested in data engineering. Include your programme, relevant skills or interests and GitHub profile. LinkedIn is optional. Display the official UTM Faculty of Computing logo without changing its proportions or colours. Do not publish private contact details.

B

Learning journey

Organise your work around the SCST1133 topics or course themes. Show connections between foundations, tools, the data landscape, industry, lifecycle, acquisition, governance, ethics and emerging areas.

C

Selected work and evidence

Present substantial artifacts from course activities and assessments. Examples include a Figma prototype, industry talk or visit work, lifecycle video, data acquisition work and project presentation materials.

D

Reflection on artifacts

For every major artifact, explain what you learned, a challenge or decision, its connection to a course concept or CLO and what you would improve. Do not merely list the steps completed.

E

Course learning summary

Summarise the knowledge and skills you gained. Explain how the course changed your understanding of data engineering and identify areas you want to develop further.

F

Sources and acknowledgements

Credit data, images, code, text, templates and other materials. State when and how digital or AI tools supported your work, following the course instructions.

04

Reflection guide

Use these questions to shape a concise, connected reflection. You do not need to present your response as a numbered list.

Contribution and purpose
What did I create or contribute, and what was its purpose?
Learning and application
What data engineering concept, tool or practice did I learn or apply?
Challenge and decision
What challenge or decision affected my work, and how did I respond?
CLO evidence
How does this work demonstrate one or more course learning outcomes?
Next improvement
What would I change, improve or investigate next?
Example artifact entry

Data Engineering Lifecycle Video · CLO2 · Video link · Explanation · Reflection · Source credits

If I revised the video, I would add a clear example showing how a data quality check affects the next stage.

Important: Use this only as a model for structure. Write your own reflection.

05

Quality and submission requirements

  • Use your own words and submit original work. Acknowledge any text, code or design created by others.
  • Keep the portfolio public and accessible to the assessor. Test every link and media item before submission.
  • Host the portfolio on GitHub Pages or another approved platform.
  • Register or update your public portfolio link using Update Profile on the SCST1133 Student Information page.
  • Use clear headings, consistent navigation and readable text. Add alternative text or captions where useful.
  • Do not publish passwords, tokens, private details, restricted datasets or another person's work without permission.
  • Update the portfolio throughout the semester so it shows your learning progress.
  • Submit the final portfolio URL through e-Learning by the announced deadline.
Your portfolio files remain on your chosen hosting platform.

The SCST1133 Student Information page stores and displays only your public portfolio link.

07

Assessment rubric

The portfolio is scored out of 100 marks. Marks are awarded according to how closely your work meets each descriptor.

CriterionMarksMeets the criterion stronglyNeeds improvement
Course evidence and completeness25Selected artifacts are substantial, clearly explained and cover the course learning journey.Evidence is limited, unclear, incomplete or weakly connected to SCST1133.
Reflection and analysis25Reflections explain learning, decisions and challenges, and identify specific improvements.Reflections are missing, mostly descriptive or provide little analysis of learning.
Understanding and CLO alignment20Explanations are accurate and explicitly connect work to data engineering concepts and relevant CLOs.Explanations include inaccuracies or do not show how work relates to course concepts or CLOs.
Organisation and navigation10Sections are easy to find, links work and content has a logical flow.Navigation is confusing, links are broken or the structure makes evidence difficult to locate.
Visual and communication quality10The design is readable, consistent and suitable for a professional learning portfolio.The design or writing makes the portfolio difficult to read or understand.
Professionalism and academic integrity10Work is original, sources and tool use are acknowledged, and privacy is protected.Sources or tool use are not acknowledged, copied work appears, or privacy is not handled responsibly.

Course-weight conversion: Portfolio mark ÷ 100 × 6. For example, 80/100 contributes 4.8 percentage points to the course total.
