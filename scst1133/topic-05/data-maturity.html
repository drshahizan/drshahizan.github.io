Data Maturity

How a data engineer’s work changes as an organisation grows in its use of data.

06Maturity
Organisational view

Starting, scaling and leading with data

Data maturity is the progression toward greater use, capability and integration of data across an organisation. It depends on how data is used as a competitive advantage—not on company age or revenue. A young start-up can be more data-mature than a century-old company.

StageWhat the organisation looks likeWhat a data engineer focuses onWatch out for
1 · Starting with dataFuzzy goals, early architecture, ad hoc requests, a very small team of generalists.Get buy-in from leaders, define a suitable architecture, audit key data and build a solid foundation.Jumping to machine learning too early; working in a silo; unnecessary technical complexity.
2 · Scaling with dataFormal data practices; roles move from generalists to specialists.Scalable, robust architectures; DevOps and DataOps practices; systems that support ML.Chasing fashionable technology; forgetting that the team, not the hardware, is the main bottleneck.
3 · Leading with dataA data-driven organisation with self-service analytics and ML.Automation for new data sources; governance, quality and DataOps; catalogues, lineage and metadata tools; collaboration.Complacency; expensive “hobby” projects that do not deliver value.

Why maturity matters for the ecosystem

The same job title can mean very different work. In a stage 1 organisation, a data engineer may also act as analyst and software engineer. In a stage 3 organisation, the role is specialised and focused on enabling others to use data safely and independently.
