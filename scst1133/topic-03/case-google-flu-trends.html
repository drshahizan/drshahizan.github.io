When big data gets it wrong

Google Flu Trends estimated influenza levels from the volume of flu-related searches. Launched in 2008, it was celebrated as a triumph of big data—until its estimates drifted far from reality.

At a glance

OrganisationGoogle
ActiveLaunched 2008; public estimates discontinued in 2015
Key findingOverestimated flu in 100 of 108 weeks from August 2011 to September 2013; at one peak it estimated 11% of the US had flu, nearly double the official 6%
Topic 03 link03 DE vs DS · E2 Big data hype

Why it went wrong

In a 2014 article in Science, Lazer and colleagues called the problem “big data hubris”—the assumption that big data can replace traditional data collection rather than complement it. They identified several causes:

  • Google’s search algorithm kept changing, so search volume changed for reasons unrelated to illness.
  • Suggested searches could influence what people typed.
  • The search terms and model were never published, so others could not check or reproduce the work.

Their conclusion: big data is most useful when combined with traditional “small data”.
