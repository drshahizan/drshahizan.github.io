Learning Activities

Five short activities that turn the eight steps into evidence of understanding.

AActivities
Class activities

Practise thinking like a data engineer

Complete each activity individually first, then compare and refine your answers with your group. Tick each item in the evidence tracker on the Overview page as you finish.

Activity 1 · Definition remix Step 01 · Individual · 10 min

Read the four perspectives

Use the table on What Is Data Engineering?.

Find the common thread

Underline the ideas that appear in more than one perspective.

Write your own definition

In one sentence of no more than 30 words, define data engineering in your own words. It must mention where data comes from and who uses the result.

Compare

Swap with a partner. What did each of you include that the other missed?

Activity 2 · Build the timeline

Step 02 · Groups of 4 · 20 min

Your lecturer will give each group sixteen event cards. Place each card in the correct era, then answer the questions.

Event cards (shuffled)
Amazon launches cloud services for external customersBill Inmon coins the term “data warehouse”
Google publishes the MapReduce paperEngineers move from batch toward event streaming
Yahoo engineers open-source Apache HadoopIBM develops the relational database and SQL
The modern data stack assembles off-the-shelf toolsCompanies run Hadoop clusters for only a few gigabytes
Google publishes the Google File System paperPrivacy laws such as the GDPR shape pipeline design
MPP databases enable scalable analytics“Big data engineers” become simply “data engineers”
Web companies such as Amazon and Yahoo appearSpark, Hive and Cassandra spread worldwide
Roles such as BI engineer and ETL developer emergeEngineers focus on security, DataOps and orchestration
  1. Which era produced the most change, in your view? Why?
  2. Find one example of “what’s old is new again”.
  3. Which event would you add from your own experience of technology?

Activity 3 · Hierarchy-of-needs triage

Steps 03 and 06 · Pairs · 15 min

Place each task on a level of the pyramid in Figure 4, then label it DE (mainly data engineering) or DS (mainly data science or analysis).

#Task
1Set up logging so every app click is recorded
2Train a model that predicts which students may drop a course
3Copy sensor readings from a factory into central storage every minute
4Remove duplicate customer records
5Run an A/B test of two website layouts
6Build a dashboard of monthly sales
7Combine attendance data with timetable data
8Schedule a nightly job and alert the team if it fails

10Convert dates from many formats into one standard format
11Fine-tune a language model for customer support

Discuss: Which tasks were hard to classify? What does that say about how the two roles overlap?

Activity 4 · Label the lifecycle

Steps 04 and 05 · Groups · 20 min

Scenario: A home has a smart electricity meter. Every 30 minutes the meter sends a reading to the utility company. The owner sees a daily usage chart in a mobile app, receives an alert when usage is unusually high and gets a monthly bill.

  1. Label the five stages: what is generated, how it is ingested, where it is stored, how it is transformed and how it is served.
  2. For each of the six undercurrents, describe one thing that could go wrong if it were ignored.
  3. Which stage would you consider the most critical for this service? Justify your answer.

Activity 5 · Balancing-act debate

Step 06 · Groups · 15 min

Half the groups represent a five-person start-up launching a food-review app. The other half represent a national bank. Rank the six goals—cost, agility, scalability, simplicity, reuse and interoperability—from most to least important for your organisation, then defend your top two and bottom two in a two-minute debate.

Take-home task · My Data Engineering Primer

Create a one-page infographic in Figma that explains data engineering to a first-year student from another faculty. Publish it on your GitHub Pages site using the skills from Topic 02.

Your primer must include:

  • your own definition (Activity 1)
  • a mini timeline with at least four milestones
  • the five lifecycle stages and six undercurrents
  • one difference between data engineering and data science
  • one future trend and why it interests you

Submit: the GitHub Pages link through UTM e-Learning.

✓

Lecturer note: Activities 1 and 3 work well during the lecture; Activities 2, 4 and 5 suit a tutorial or lab session.
