Required case study 1

Uber: Four Generations of a Data Platform

From a few terabytes in a warehouse to more than 100 petabytes with near-real-time freshness.

C1Uber
Required · Evolution and lifecycle

History repeats inside one company

In 2018, Uber’s Hadoop platform team described how their data platform had evolved. The story mirrors the four eras of data engineering from Step 02—compressed into just a few years.

At a glance

OrganisationUber (ride-hailing and delivery)
PeriodBefore 2014 to 2018 and beyond
Starting pointA few terabytes in separate operational databases
2018 scaleMore than 100 petabytes; around 100,000 Presto queries per day
Topic 03 link02 Evolution · 04 Lifecycle · 06 Balancing act

The four generations

GenerationApproachWhat it solvedWhat it created
1 · Data warehouseData copied from operational databases into a Vertica warehouse with ad hoc ETL jobs and SQL accessOne central, global view of the business instead of scattered databasesFragile ingestion of unvalidated JSON, high costs, duplicated data, and a warehouse also acting as a data lake
2 · Hadoop data lakeRaw data ingested once into HDFS, stored in columnar Parquet, queried with Presto, Spark and HiveHorizontal scale to tens of petabytes; schemas enforced; less load on source databasesNew data took about 24 hours to arrive; snapshot ingestion was slow; no updates or deletes
3 · Incremental platform (2017)Apache Hudi and Kafka changelogs ingest only what changedFreshness improved from 24+ hours to under one hour; updates and deletes supportedRemaining limits in data quality checks and storage efficiency
4 · Next generation (planned)Schema and quality checks at the source; target of minutes, not hoursBetter data quality and efficiency—

Why this case matters

  • It shows the evolution from warehouse → big data → modern incremental platforms in a single organisation.
  • Every generation touched all five lifecycle stages, not only storage.
  • Each design was a trade-off: gaining scalability cost freshness; gaining freshness required new tools and skills.
  • Undercurrents such as data management (schema enforcement, quality) became more important as the platform grew—exactly as FDE predicts.

Analyse with the Topic 03 lens

  1. Match each Uber generation to one of the four eras on the Evolution page. Where does the match break down?
  2. For Generation 2, describe what happens at each of the five lifecycle stages.
  3. Which undercurrent was weakest in Generation 1? Give evidence.
  4. Using the balancing act, explain the trade-off between scalability and freshness in Generations 2 and 3.
  5. If you were Uber’s data engineer in 2014, what would you have done differently, knowing what you know now?
