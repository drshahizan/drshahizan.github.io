Step 03 · Distinguish

Data Engineering and Data Science

Separate disciplines that depend on each other: one gets the data, the other gets value from it.

03DE and DS
Core concept

Upstream and downstream

Some argue that data engineering is part of data science. Reis and Housley disagree: the two are complementary but distinctly different, and data engineering sits upstream.

The Data Science Hierarchy of Needs

In 2017, Monica Rogati arranged data work as a pyramid, similar to Maslow’s hierarchy. The lower levels—collecting data, moving and storing it, and exploring and transforming it—must be in place before the upper levels of analytics, experimentation and AI can work.

Data scientists are estimated to spend 70–80% of their time on the bottom three levels: gathering, cleaning and processing data. Ideally, they would spend more than 90% of their time at the top of the pyramid. Data engineers make that possible by building a solid foundation.

“Garbage in, garbage out.” Even the most advanced model fails if the data beneath it is incomplete, late or inconsistent. Data engineering is equal in importance to data science, not a support act.
