From two papers to a global ecosystem

Reis and Housley describe the Google papers as a “big bang” for data technologies and for the culture of data engineering.

YearMilestoneBig idea
2003The Google File System (Ghemawat, Gobioff & Leung)Store huge files across many cheap machines, expecting some to fail.
2004MapReduce (Dean & Ghemawat)Process huge data by splitting work into map and reduce steps that run in parallel.
2006Apache Hadoop open-sourced by engineers at YahooAnyone could now use these ideas on their own clusters.

MapReduce in plain words

Imagine counting how often every word appears in all the books in a library. One person would take years. Instead:

  1. Split the books among many helpers.
  2. Map: each helper counts the words in their own books.
  3. Shuffle: all counts for the same word are sent to the same person.
  4. Reduce: that person adds them up to get the total.

If one helper falls ill, their books are simply given to someone else. Designing for failure became a core principle of data engineering.
