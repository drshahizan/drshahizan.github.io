Public Health England: 15,841 Missing Cases

A pipeline that relied on an old spreadsheet format silently dropped thousands of positive test results.

O1PHE
Optional · Undercurrents

When a spreadsheet becomes critical infrastructure

In October 2020, England’s public-health agency revealed that 15,841 positive COVID-19 cases from 25 September to 2 October had not been included in daily figures or passed promptly to contact tracers.

At a glance

OrganisationPublic Health England
When25 September – 2 October 2020
Impact15,841 cases unreported; contact tracing delayed
Topic 03 link05 Undercurrents · 08 Dark matter data

What happened

Generation and ingestion

Commercial laboratories sent test results as CSV files, which were fetched automatically.

Transformation in a spreadsheet

Results were loaded into templates in Excel’s older XLS format, which allows a maximum of 65,536 rows per sheet. Each result used several rows, so each template could hold only about 1,400 cases.

Silent failure

Records beyond the limit were simply left out—no error, no alert.

Fix

Large files were split into smaller ones as an immediate measure, and a full review of the system was ordered.

Lessons for data engineers

  • Data management: check record counts between stages—what goes in should come out.
  • DataOps: monitoring and alerting would have caught the drop immediately.
  • Dark matter data: spreadsheets are powerful but were never designed to be critical pipelines.

Analyse with the Topic 03 lens

  1. Which lifecycle stage failed?
  2. Which two undercurrents would have prevented the problem? How?
  3. Design one automated check that would have caught the error.
