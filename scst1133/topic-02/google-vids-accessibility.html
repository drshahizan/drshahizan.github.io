Captions and Video Accessibility

Make the assignment understandable to viewers with different hearing, visual, language and learning needs.

Accessibility requirements

Accurate captions

Correct terminology, names, punctuation and timing rather than accepting automatic output without review.

Readable text

Use large type, short lines and safe placement away from interface controls.

Strong contrast

Separate text from the background and do not communicate meaning through colour alone.

Clear narration

Use a steady pace, appropriate volume and minimal background noise.

Descriptive context

Explain important visual changes and provide alt text where the tool supports it.

Full review

Watch the final version with captions enabled and audio both on and off.

Caption quality check

Generate or add captions

Allow processing to complete before beginning the review.

Correct the transcript

Pay particular attention to course codes, tool names and technical vocabulary.

Style for readability

Choose suitable typography, position and contrast without covering evidence.

Test the complete video

Confirm synchronisation and ensure each caption remains on screen long enough.

Final accessibility checklist

  • Captions match the final audio.
  • On-screen text can be read at normal playback size.
  • Visuals remain understandable without colour alone.
  • Narration is audible on ordinary speakers.
  • No rapid flashing or distracting movement is present.
  • A transcript can be supplied if requested.
