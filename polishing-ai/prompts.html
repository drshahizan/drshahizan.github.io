Before you paste

Do not paste sensitive data or participant information. Check every output, especially facts, figures and references.

Polish without changing meaning

For a paragraph whose ideas are complete but whose language needs work.

Act as an academic editor for a Q1 journal in [FIELD].
Improve the clarity, grammar and flow of the paragraph below.
Rules:
- Do not change the meaning or add new claims, data or citations.
- Keep technical terms exactly as written.
- Use [British/American] English.
Return: (1) the revised paragraph, (2) a short table listing each change and why.

Ensures every essential element of the abstract is present.

1. Identify any missing or weak element.
2. Suggest a revised abstract within [WORD LIMIT] words.
3. Do not invent results; use only the information in my text.

Research gap in the introduction

Makes the gap and contribution explicit.

Read my introduction and answer:
1. What research gap does it claim? Quote the sentence.
2. Is the gap supported by cited literature, or only asserted?
3. Are the objectives and contributions stated explicitly?
Then suggest how to restructure the final two paragraphs so the gap leads directly to the objectives.
Do not add citations.

From summary to synthesis

Rewrites “Author A found… Author B found…” paragraphs.

The paragraph below summarises studies one by one.
Rewrite it as a synthesis organised by theme or argument, not by author.
Keep every citation exactly as given and do not add new sources.
Highlight any point where the studies disagree.

Alignment check

Tests consistency from objectives to conclusions.

Below are my research objectives, research questions, main findings and conclusion.
Build a table that maps each objective to its research question, finding and conclusion.
Flag any objective without a finding, any finding without an objective, and any conclusion not supported by the findings.

Reverse outline

Reveals the overall flow of the argument.

Summarise each paragraph of the text below in one sentence, numbered.
Then tell me:
- which paragraphs repeat the same point,
- where the logical flow breaks,
- which paragraph could be cut with the least loss.

Hedging and overclaiming

Finds claims stronger than the evidence allows.

Identify every sentence in the text below that makes a claim stronger than the evidence supports (e.g., "proves", "clearly shows", "always").
For each, suggest a more appropriately hedged version.
Do not change sentences that are already appropriately cautious.

Remove AI-sounding style

Strips clichés and repetitive phrasing.

Review the text below for wording that sounds generic or machine-generated: overused words, filler transitions, inflated adjectives and repetitive sentence openings.
List each instance with a plainer alternative.
Keep my original sentence structure where possible.

Demanding reviewer simulation

Anticipates criticism before submission.

Act as a critical but fair reviewer for [JOURNAL NAME], a Q1 journal in [FIELD].
Evaluate the manuscript summary below on: novelty, theoretical contribution, methodological rigour, clarity and fit with the journal scope.
Give: (1) major concerns, (2) minor concerns, (3) a likely recommendation (accept / minor / major / reject) with justification.

Response to reviewers

Structures a reply to each reviewer comment.

Help me draft a point-by-point response to the reviewer comment below.
Tone: respectful, concise and confident.
Structure: thank the reviewer, state what was changed (with page/line placeholders), and explain briefly.
If I disagree, phrase it politely with evidence.

Cover letter

A first draft to tailor yourself.

Draft a cover letter to the editor of [JOURNAL NAME] for the manuscript "[TITLE]".
Include: the problem addressed, the main contribution in 2 sentences, why it fits the journal scope, confirmation that it is original and not under review elsewhere.
Maximum 250 words. Avoid exaggerated language.

AI disclosure statement

An accurate, concise disclosure.

Write a short AI-use disclosure statement for a journal manuscript.
Facts:
- Tool(s) used: [TOOL AND VERSION]
- Purpose: [e.g., language editing and readability of selected sections]
- Sections affected: [SECTIONS]
- Authors reviewed and edited all output and take full responsibility.
Keep it under 70 words and neutral in tone.

Journal-fit evaluator

Review perspective 1 of 5: is the manuscript suitable for the target journal?

Act as the editor-in-chief of [JOURNAL NAME]. Read the journal's aims and scope below and my abstract.
1. Is this manuscript within scope? Quote the scope statement that supports or rules it out.
2. Would readers of this journal care about the contribution? Why or why not?
3. Name the single biggest risk of desk rejection.
Do not rewrite the abstract.

Methodology reviewer

Review perspective 2 of 5: are the methods sound, appropriate and sufficiently described?

Act as a methodology reviewer in [FIELD]. Read my Methods section.
List (1) anything another researcher would need in order to replicate the study but cannot find, (2) design choices that need justification, (3) threats to validity I have not acknowledged.
Do not suggest analyses I did not do unless you label them as optional.
