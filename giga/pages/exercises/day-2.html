DAY 02 · TELL THE STORY

Plan a short video.
Make the message clear.

Move from a client brief to an edited short video: define the audience, draft a script, plan scenes, assemble the video, add captions and prepare a simple showcase.

Use the fictional case accurately. Confirmed information: Mersing Coastal Homestay is family-run, is located in Mersing, Johor, and offers stays and local experiences. Do not show or claim unconfirmed facilities, prices, activities, availability, reviews or contact details.
ACTIVITY 1 · BRIEF AND AUDIENCE

Choose one viewer and one message

A short video works best when it has one audience, one key message and one clear next step.

  1. Select an audience: families, couples or groups of friends.
  2. Choose the goal: introduce the homestay case or invite an enquiry.
  3. Write the key message in one sentence.
  4. Use a placeholder for any booking or contact detail that is not supplied.
Prompt to copy · Prepare a video brief
Prepare a short video brief for a fictional family-run homestay in Mersing, Johor. Confirmed offer: stays and local experiences. Intended audience: [families / couples / groups of friends]. Goal: [introduce the homestay / invite an enquiry]. Return: audience need, one key message, one call to action, suggested video length between 20 and 30 seconds, and a list of facts that still need confirmation. Do not invent facilities, prices, activities or contact details.
Expected outputA one-paragraph video brief with a selected audience, one message, a 20–30 second target duration and an enquiry call to action using a placeholder.
ACTIVITY 2 · SCRIPT

Write a concise voice-over

Ask a text tool for several options, select one and read it aloud. Aim for simple language that fits the time limit.

  1. Keep the script to about 45–65 spoken words for a short video.
  2. Use only confirmed case facts.
  3. Leave a pause for visuals and avoid overloading the viewer with text.
  4. Read it aloud and shorten any awkward sentences.
Prompt to copy · Draft the voice-over
Write three warm, clear voice-over script options for a 20–30 second promotional video. Case facts: fictional family-run homestay; Mersing, Johor; offers stays and local experiences. Audience: [insert selected audience]. Goal: [insert selected goal]. Each script must be 45–65 words, use only these facts, avoid claims about facilities, prices, specific activities, availability or reviews, and end with “Find out more: [enquiry details]”. Label any information that needs confirmation.
Expected outputThree short scripts. Select one, verify every claim, and save the approved draft in your Day 2 folder.
ACTIVITY 3 · STORYBOARD

Turn the script into a scene plan

Plan visuals before opening the video editor. A storyboard helps you match each scene to the voice-over and prevents unsupported claims.

  1. Break the script into four to six scenes.
  2. For each scene, specify the visual, on-screen text, narration and approximate duration.
  3. Use general coastal or Mersing-inspired visuals only as illustrative concepts.
  4. Do not represent a generated scene as a photograph of the real homestay.
Prompt to copy · Create a storyboard
Turn this approved 20–30 second voice-over into a storyboard with 4–6 scenes. Return a table with columns Scene number, Duration in seconds, Visual direction, On-screen text, Voice-over line and Asset needed. Keep total duration between 20 and 30 seconds. Use only confirmed claims. Describe visuals as illustrative concepts; do not imply they show the actual homestay. Script: [paste your approved script]
Expected outputA scene-by-scene table whose durations add up to 20–30 seconds, with a visual, caption and voice-over for each scene.
ACTIVITY 4 · ASSEMBLE AND EDIT

Create, edit and export the video

Use an available video editor or approved video-generation tool. If a feature is unavailable, assemble the scenes in a simple editor and use a recorded voice-over or text captions.

  1. Create or select visuals for each storyboard scene.
  2. Import the visuals and arrange them in storyboard order.
  3. Add the approved voice-over, readable captions, a title and the placeholder call to action.
  4. Check pacing, spelling, audio level, contrast and factual accuracy.
  5. Export a shareable video file and keep the editable project if the tool allows.
Prompt to copy · Generate a scene visual
Create an illustrative, realistic travel-promotion visual for a short video about Mersing, Johor. Show [describe a general coastal or local-experience scene, not a real homestay property]. Warm natural daylight, calm welcoming mood, uncluttered composition, horizontal 16:9 frame, no text, no logo, no identifiable people, no claims about accommodation facilities. This is a concept image and must not be presented as a photograph of Mersing Coastal Homestay.
Expected outputA short edited video with a clear beginning, middle and end; readable captions; balanced audio; and no unsupported business claims. If export is unavailable, save the storyboard and a preview of the edited sequence.
ACTIVITY 5 · SHOWCASE

Present the video and explain your choices

Prepare a brief share-out that connects your creative decisions to the audience and the service goal.

  1. Show the video or storyboard preview.
  2. State the selected audience and key message.
  3. Explain one design or editing choice.
  4. Name one fact you deliberately left as a placeholder.
  5. Share one improvement you would make after feedback.
Prompt to copy · Prepare a 60-second showcase
Help me prepare a 60-second presentation about my short promotional video. Use these notes: audience [audience], goal [goal], key message [message], one visual/editing choice [choice], one placeholder retained because the fact is unconfirmed [placeholder], and one possible improvement [improvement]. Use plain, confident language. Do not invent facts.
Expected outputA short, understandable explanation of who the video is for, what it communicates and how you kept it accurate.
