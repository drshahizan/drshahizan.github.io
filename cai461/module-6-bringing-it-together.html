A four-layer evaluation framework

Every module filled one layer. Read the stack from the bottom up: each layer assumes the one below it works, and adds questions the lower layer cannot answer.

Layer 4: Trust and deploymentpass^k reliability · cost · safety and policy violations · failure modes (MAST) Layer 3: Systemlatency · throughput · scalability curves · robustness to failures Layer 2: Interactionsocial welfare · Pareto efficiency · stability · price of anarchy · messages per task Layer 1: Individual agentperformance measure · expected utility · task success P(Ψ)

Lower layers are necessary but not sufficient: a system of rational agents can still be slow, unstable or unreliable.

LayerAskPick at least one

Case activity: design an evaluation plan

Your group takes one case:

  • Case A, PNU Smart Shuttle: 8 shuttle agents bid for ride requests across campus using Contract Net.
  • Case B, Campus Evacuation Model: a NetLogo model of 3,000 students leaving buildings during a fire drill.
  • Case C, Course-Advising Crew: three LLM agents (planner, retriever, checker) answer students’ registration questions.

8 minutes in breakout rooms, then 4 minutes for two groups to share.

Each group delivers one slide or one chat message

  1. One metric from each of the four layers
  2. One baseline to compare against
  3. One experiment: what you vary and how many runs
  4. What you will trace or log
  5. One likely failure mode and how you would detect it

Key takeaways

  1. Decide what “good” means first. Measure the environment, not the agent’s behaviour.
  2. Expected utility and success probability are the foundation. Modern benchmarks still estimate them.
  3. For societies of agents, report welfare and stability together. The price of anarchy shows the cost of selfishness.
  4. Measure curves, not points. Scalability and robustness need repeated, controlled experiments.
  5. For models, verify, validate and replicate, and report with ODD.
  6. For LLM agents, add reliability (pass^k), cost, failure modes and validated judges, all built on tracing.

Questions for discussion

Choosing the utility

In the shuttle case, is a late ride worth half a successful one, or nothing? Who should decide: the students, the university or the designer?

When is a coordinator worth it?

If a central allocator has a price of anarchy of 1 but is a single point of failure, how would you weigh the two in one report?

Trusting a judge

How many human-labelled examples would you need before you trust an LLM judge for course advice, and what agreement level would you accept?

Benchmarks and the real world

An agent scores 90% on a benchmark but students still complain. Which layer of the framework did the benchmark miss?

Self-check quiz

Choose an answer for each question, then check. Every answer comes with a short explanation.

1. Which layer does “latency grows sharply after 80 agents” belong to?

Latency and scalability curves are system-level measurements.

2. Why are lower layers “necessary but not sufficient”?

Each layer adds questions that the layer below cannot answer.

3. In the case activity, what must every group include besides metrics?

The five deliverables are metrics per layer, baseline, experiment, tracing plan and a failure mode with detection.

4. In the lab, which two designs do you compare?

The lab compares a sequential chain with a supervisor, or star, topology.
